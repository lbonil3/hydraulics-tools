    Sources & method

    1. Which bids. Low-bidder unit prices for the bid code. Houston District bids are used when there are at least 5; otherwise statewide (labeled). You can force either region.
    2. Base price. The median low bid of jobs within 3x of your quantity. If fewer than 5 jobs are that close, the 5 jobs closest in size are used. Far outliers (beyond 3x the interquartile range on a log scale) are left out.
    3. Quantity adjustment. A grouped price-vs-quantity slope (log price vs log quantity) that borrows strength from related codes: this code's own slope is shrunk toward the slope of its Item family (same spec item and unit), which is shrunk toward the slope across all TxDOT items. Each level counts like its number of bids against 10 bids' worth of the level above, so a code with 300 bids mostly keeps its own slope and a code with 10 bids is about half its own, half borrowed. The comment shows the shares. Beyond the sizes on record a slope that says bigger jobs cost more is never extrapolated (the family or all-items slope is used, labeled assumed). The adjustment never lowers the base more than 25% or raises it more than 25%, never goes above the 90th percentile, and inside the data never goes below the 10th percentile.
    4. Range. Low and high scale the suggestion by the 25th and 75th percentiles relative to the median. The range is widened 15% each way when extrapolating, and to at least −40% / +50% with fewer than 5 bids.
    5. Confidence. High / Medium / Low from the number of bids, whether your quantity is inside the sizes on record, how spread out prices are, and whether there are bids in the last 12 months. More than 2x beyond the largest (or below half the smallest) job is labeled extrapolated beyond data and always Low.
    6. Escalation (optional). With an expected letting date, prices are moved from the median let date of the bids used to that date with the FHWA National Highway Construction Cost Index (NHCCI, seasonally adjusted, data.transportation.gov r94d-n4f9; FHWA NHCCI page). The NHCCI is national, not Texas-specific. Past the latest published quarter, your %/yr (default 0%) is used and labeled user input, assumed.
    7. Units. You can enter a quantity in another unit only when the conversion is exact: LF ↔ STA (100) ↔ MI (5,280), SF ↔ SY (9) ↔ AC (43,560). Prices stay per the unit TxDOT bid; the comment shows the conversion. Nothing else is converted, and the data build checks that no code mixes units.
    8. Often bid with. Codes that appear on the same projects in the data: ranked by the share of this code's projects that also bid them, kept only when they share at least 3 projects and co-occur at least 2x as often as chance (so items on nearly every job don't crowd the list). "Typical qty" is the median ratio of the two quantities on shared projects. Pressing a code pre-fills the form with that ratio-based quantity for you to check. This is a data pattern, not engineering advice.
    9. Notes & printing. Each item has a notes line that goes into Copy table, the CSV and the one-page printable summary (Print summary), together with the backup sentences, total, print date and data range.
    10. Possibly unbalanced bids are flagged, never removed. Every flagged bid stays in every statistic and in the suggested price; the page shows the median and range with and without them for information only. A low bid is flagged when any of these hold (thresholds in flags.js):
      • Install/remove pair — on the same project, an install item and its matching remove item (same spec item, same description without the install/remove words, e.g. 5002-7001/7002, 506-7039/7041): remove priced at or above install, or the remove/install ratio more than 3x (or under 1/3) the typical ratio for that pair, while install + remove stays within 0.5x–2x of the typical combined price (money moved between the halves). Typical values need at least 3 jobs of the pair; otherwise only the remove ≥ install test applies.
      • Far from the other bidders — low bid under 25% or over 250% of the median of the other bidders' prices for that item on the same job (at least 2 other bids).
      • Placeholder price — $0.01 or less, or $1.00 or less when that is under 5% of the code's statewide median.
    11. Backup sentence. Built only from the numbers shown. Assumed values are marked (assumed).

    What it does not know: project-specific conditions (access, water depth, traffic control, phasing), escalation after the bid date, how a contractor spread mobilization or overhead across items (unbalanced bids), and anything not in the TxDOT bid tabulations. It is planning-level only; ESBD / Electronic Bidding System remain official.

    Source: Texas Open Data Portal, TxDOT Bid Tabulations (dataset de7b-7dna), low bidder rank = 1, plus all-bidder count and average per job. Item titles from TxDOT 2024 Standard Specifications (via Bid Cost). Data regenerated with bid-estimator/scripts/fetch-bids.js and build-data.js.
